Ecclesiastes 6

The Preacher describes the evil of a man to whom God gives wealth, honor, and everything he desires, yet God does not give him the power to enjoy it. He argues that a stillborn child is better off than such a man, and that no one knows what is good for man in the days of his vain life.

vKing James VersionModern English
1There is an evil which I have seen under the sun, and it is common among men:There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it is common among men:
2A man to whom God hath given riches, wealth, and honour, so that he wanteth nothing for his soul of all that he desireth, yet God giveth him not power to eat thereof, but a stranger eateth it: this is vanity, and it is an evil disease.A man to whom God has given riches, wealth, and honor, so that He lacks nothing for His soul of all that He desires, yet God does not give Him the power to enjoy them, but a stranger eats them. This is vanity, and it is an evil disease.
3If a man beget an hundred children, and live many years, so that the days of his years be many, and his soul be not filled with good, and also that he have no burial; I say, that an untimely birth is better than he.If a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years, so that the days of his years are many, but his soul is not filled with good, and he has no burial — I say that a stillborn child is better than he.
4For he cometh in with vanity, and departeth in darkness, and his name shall be covered with darkness.For it comes in with vanity and departs in darkness, and its name is covered with darkness.
5Moreover he hath not seen the sun, nor known any thing: this hath more rest than the other.Moreover it has not seen the sun, nor known anything. This has more rest than the other.
6Yea, though he live a thousand years twice told, yet hath he seen no good: do not all go to one place?Indeed, though he lives a thousand years twice over, yet he has seen no good. Do not all go to one place?
7All the labour of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is not filled.All the labor of man is for his mouth, and yet his appetite is not filled.
8For what hath the wise more than the fool? what hath the poor, that knoweth to walk before the living?For what does the wise have more than the fool? What does the poor man have, who knows how to walk before the living?
9Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the desire: this is also vanity and vexation of spirit.Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of desire. This also is vanity and vexation of spirit.
10That which hath been is named already, and it is known that it is man: neither can he contend with him that is mightier than he.That which has been is named already, and it is known that it is man. He cannot contend with him who is mightier than he.
11Seeing there be many things that increase vanity, what is man the better?Since there are many things that increase vanity, what is man the better for it?
12For who knoweth what is good for man in this life, all the days of his vain life which he spendeth as a shadow? for who can tell a man what shall be after him under the sun?For who knows what is good for man in this life, all the days of his vain life which he spends like a shadow? Who can tell a man what shall be after him under the sun?
